> In short: a website or landing page closely connected to **how your business works**.

Unlike a brochure site, a web platform includes technology that supports your actual work. It might help with sales or power an internal application:

* A brochure site presents a company, its services, location, and contact details.
* A growth marketing site supports commercial activity and makes it measurable.
* An e-commerce site handles sales directly.
* A web platform has features built to maintain customer relationships, integrates with your CRM or ERP, or lives entirely inside an **intranet**.

I still remember a marketing lesson from more than 23 years ago:

> A site is a showcase when it describes the business. It can also be a tool that supports the business throughout the sales process, from acquisition to retention. — ***Roberto Maldacea***

**Why does that matter?** Because a visit is the first step, the first **"yes"** from a ***potential customer***. Someone can browse your site and leave without ever becoming a customer, as most visitors do.

A **potential customer** arriving for the first time knows why they came. They **may still be choosing** between competitors, or may have already studied the available offers.

> A brochure site lets a potential customer arrive, browse, and leave. **Adios**.

A site built to support sales can use technology to keep improving the journey toward a purchase. **That is the difference**.

A web platform is a set of services and technologies that support this process. It could include a cookie, a database, or email marketing closely connected to a brochure site.
